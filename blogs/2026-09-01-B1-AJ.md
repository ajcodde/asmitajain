# Do we invent for sustenance or for utopia?

If the goal that motivates invention is achieving utopia, why aren’t we less miserable now than ever before? What does utopia even mean? Infinite happiness? That seems unsustainable, boring even, just like they showed on *The Good Place*. Emotions are chemicals, fleeting, and not built for permanence. As Patty (Hypatia) says on *The Good Place,*

> *“When perfection goes on forever, you become this glassy-eyed, mush person.”*
> 

From my perspective, utopia isn’t about unbroken happiness. It’s a world where we can pursue our passion — intellectual or otherwise — without being weighed down by survival anxieties. For me, that passion is the search for life beyond Earth. I want to study microbes that thrive in environments hostile to humans, because in them I see a path toward understanding both extraterrestrial survival and the fragility of our own planet. I’m unsure whether this desire comes from a place of fascination or the fear of Earth’s eventual decline. Perhaps both.

But even here, contradictions arise. Everyone has a different vision of utopia, and my desires may clash not only with others’ but also with themselves. If utopia requires me to give up a passion that doesn’t fit, is it still utopia? If striving for utopia always generates new problems — climate, health and AI crises — then, maybe the very desire to achieve perfection is pointless.

Maybe that’s the paradox — utopia requires conflict. Negative emotions could be part of the balance that keeps us human. If we ever abandoned that struggle and reached a place where there is no desire to improve, would that not make us lose our humanity?
